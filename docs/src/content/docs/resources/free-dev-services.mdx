A freshly set up workstation usually needs more than desktop software: somewhere to host a side project, a managed database, a CI runner, log storage, a status page. A great deal of that is available at no cost, but the offers move constantly and the word "free" covers everything from a permanent allowance to a two-week trial.

The best-maintained public index of these offers is **free-for.dev**, curated by R.I. Pienaar with contributions from more than 1,600 people. It lists hundreds of as-a-service offerings that have a genuine free tier, with the limits noted per entry.

- **Browse it:** [free-for.dev](https://free-for.dev/)
- **Source and contributions:** [ripienaar/free-for-dev](https://github.com/ripienaar/free-for-dev)

:::note[Why this page links out instead of reproducing the list]
That repository carries no licence of its own, so nobody has been granted the right to republish its text. WinUtil Pro links to it and leaves the credit, and the hosting, with its maintainers. Please send corrections and new services to them rather than here.
:::

## Telling a free tier from a trial

"Free" is used loosely. Before a service becomes part of your setup, work out which of these it actually is:

- **Genuinely free** — a permanent allowance with no card required. Safe to build on for personal projects.
- **Free tier with limits** — permanent, but capped on requests, seats, build minutes, storage, or retention. Find out what happens when you hit the cap: throttling is an inconvenience, deletion is not.
- **Trial** — free for a period, then paid. Fine for evaluating, risky as a dependency.
- **Free for open source, students, or small teams** — conditional on eligibility you must keep meeting.
- **Self-hosted** — free software you run yourself, where the cost is your server and your time.

A few checks that save trouble later:

1. Confirm the current limits on the vendor's own pricing page. A community list is a starting point, never the contract.
2. Note whether a payment card is required to start, and whether overage is billed automatically or simply blocked.
3. Check the data export path before you put anything important in. A service you cannot leave is not cheap at any price.
4. Check retention, not just volume, for anything storing logs or metrics.
5. Re-check the services you depend on about once a year. Free tiers get reduced, and projects get acquired or shut down.
